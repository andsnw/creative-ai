# AI directory pattern research — September 2026

This note records product patterns observed on public AI-tool directories and how Creative AI may adapt them without copying branding, proprietary text, rankings, code, or assets.

## FutureTools

Observed patterns:
- Search-first tool database with availability filters.
- A curated-picks concept alongside the full archive.
- A dedicated Newly Added view.
- A Recently Removed section with removal reasons.
- A submission form that is reviewed before inclusion.

Creative AI adaptation:
- Keep broad catalog access, but add an evidence-backed **Official-source reviewed** layer instead of subjective or opaque popularity ranking.
- Keep truthful lifecycle tracking and make removals auditable.
- Tighten submissions so an official source and concrete user value are required.

## Futurepedia

Observed patterns:
- Strong category-led discovery.
- Broad top-level groups with narrower subtopics.
- Category counts and task-oriented browsing.
- Tool discovery is paired with educational context and comparisons.

Creative AI adaptation:
- Preserve category pages but move toward fewer, clearer parent groups with useful subcategories.
- Show quality/trust state alongside category discovery instead of treating every listing as equally reviewed.

## Toolify

Observed patterns:
- Extremely deep taxonomy and many category entry points.
- Large catalog breadth with category counts.
- Featured placements are visually distinct from ordinary directory entries.

Creative AI adaptation:
- Borrow taxonomy depth only where it improves navigation; do not chase raw listing count.
- Keep any editorial/reviewed state explicit and evidence-based.
- Avoid sponsored-looking or popularity-style prominence unless the underlying signal is real and disclosed.

## Product rule

Creative AI should combine breadth with a visible trust model:

1. **Catalog** — all maintained entries that have passed the minimum acceptance policy.
2. **Official-source reviewed** — entries manually checked against first-party evidence and concrete user value.
3. **Pending audit** — legacy entries not yet re-reviewed under the stricter policy; this does not mean unsafe or low quality, only not yet re-audited.
4. **Removed archive** — entries removed for dead links, discontinuation, misleading identity, duplication, or failure to meet the quality policy.

Third-party directories are discovery inputs only. They are never sufficient evidence by themselves.
